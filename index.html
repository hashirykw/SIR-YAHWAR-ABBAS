f. 04 — The method

One question, five steps.

Most Physics marks are lost before the arithmetic starts: data missed, no diagram, the wrong equation, units dropped. This is the route Sir Yawar drills. Follow one braking-car question through it.

  1. 01

    Read for the data

    Underline every number and its unit, and the word that hides one: “comes to rest” means the final speed is zero.

    5054 · 1.2 Motion
  2. 02

    Draw it

    A quick free-body diagram shows which way the resultant force acts before you write a single equation.

    5054 · 1.5.1 Forces
  3. 03

    Choose the equation

    Match what you know to what you need. Acceleration first, then force, then distance.

    9702 · 2.1 Equations of motion
  4. 04

    Substitute with units

    Numbers go in with their units on every line, so a slip shows up straight away and method marks are safe.

    9702 · 1.2 SI units
  5. 05

    Check and state

    Sensible size, right sign, right significant figures, direction stated. Then underline the answer.

    9702 · 1.3 Uncertainties
Worked example
Question 3[5 marks]
Paper 2

A car of mass 1200 kg is moving at 20 m/s. The driver brakes and the car comes to rest in 4.0 s. Calculate the braking force and the distance travelled while braking.

QuantitySymbolValue
Massm1200 kg
Initial speedu20 m/s
Final speed (“comes to rest”)v0 m/s
Timet4.0 s
Free-body diagramwhile braking

Weight and the normal reaction cancel. The only unbalanced force is the braking force, acting backwards.

Pick the equationsknow → need
1a = (v − u) / tu, v, t known → a
2F = mam, a known → F
3s = ½(u + v) tu, v, t known → s

Each equation uses only quantities you already have. No equation is chosen because it “looks right”.
