Change a number. Watch the physics move.

Nine simulations for the ideas Cambridge papers test every series, plus a multiple-choice drill and flashcards. Each tool shows the working with units, the way a mark scheme expects it.

Tool 01 · 5054 1.2 · 0625 1.2 · 9702 2.1

Motion grapher

Set a starting speed, an acceleration and a time. The speed–time graph draws itself: its gradient is the acceleration and the shaded area is the distance. Press run to watch the car do it.

Tool 02 · 5054 1.5, 1.6 · 9702 3.1–3.3

Forces & momentum

Find the resultant force and the acceleration from a free-body diagram, or collide two trolleys and check that momentum is conserved while kinetic energy may not be.

Tool 03 · 5054 1.7 · 9702 5.1, 5.2

Energy & efficiency

A mass slides down from a height. Choose how much energy friction wastes and read the stores, the speed at the bottom and the efficiency, with a Sankey diagram drawn to scale.

Tool 04 · 5054 2.2.2 · 9702 14.3

Specific heat capacity

Heat a block or a liquid with an electric heater. Energy in is power × time, and the temperature rise depends on the mass and the material. Compare materials on one graph.

Tool 05 · 5054 3.1, 4.6 · 9702 7.1

Waves & the oscilloscope

Change the frequency and the wave speed and watch the wavelength respond, v = fλ. The oscilloscope below shows the same wave against time, so you can read the period from the timebase.

Tool 06 · 5054 3.2.2 · 0625 3.2.2

Refraction & critical angle

Send a ray across a boundary. Going into a denser medium it bends towards the normal. Going out of one, past the critical angle, all of it reflects: total internal reflection.

Tool 07 · 5054 3.2.3 · 0625 3.2.3

Thin lens ray diagram

Move the object along the axis of a converging lens. The two standard rays are drawn for you; where they cross is the image. Inside the focal length the image turns virtual: a magnifying glass.

Tool 08 · 5054 4.2–4.4 · 9702 9, 10

Circuit solver

Up to three resistors in series or in parallel with a cell. Get the combined resistance, the current, the p.d. and power for each resistor, and the effect of internal resistance at AS.

Tool 09 · 5054 5.2 · 9702 23.2

Half-life simulator

Every nucleus has the same chance of decaying each second, but which one goes next is random. Run it and watch the random dots add up to a smooth exponential curve.
